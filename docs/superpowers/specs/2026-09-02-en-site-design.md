# The rest of the site in English

**Date:** 2026-09-02
**Slice:** 2 of 2. It depends on [slice 1](./2026-09-02-cv-en-pdf-design.md),
which brings the English dataset, the message dictionary, the locale-aware
formatters, the staleness lock and `/en/cv`. None of the work below is possible
before that lands, and none of it is needed for the English PDF to work.

## 1. What this delivers

- `/en/` — the landing in English: hero, index, knowledge map, projects and the
  full CV, the same single page `/` is.
- `/en/cv.json` and `/en/llms.txt` — the two surfaces agents consume.
- `public/og.en.jpg` — the social card for `/en/`.
- `hreflang` + `x-default` on both landings, which `docs/09` §2.3 already
  declares mandatory "the day `content.en.json` exists".
- A visible ES/EN switch.

## 2. The pieces, and what each one costs

### 2.1 The landing

`src/pages/index.astro` is the largest page in the repo and the only one that
ships JavaScript. It is not copied. The page becomes a component that takes a
locale; `src/pages/index.astro` and `src/pages/en/index.astro` are two thin
callers, the same shape slice 1 gives `cv.astro`.

The anchors are English on the English page: `#map`, `#projects`, `#cv`. They are
addresses, and an address in a language the reader does not speak is noise. This
is the one place where the split is not free — `lab-hover-css.ts` and
`single-landing.check.ts` both name the Spanish anchors today, and both learn to
take them from one table rather than from a literal.

The map itself needs almost nothing: node labels come from the dataset (English
in the English dataset), and `graph-layout.ts` is deterministic and language-blind.
`src/lib/map-labels.ts` measures sizes, not words.

**Watch:** `bundle-budget.check.ts` counts bytes per page. Two landings means the
budget applies twice, and the shared chunk should stay shared — if the locale
split makes Rollup emit two copies of the 3D bundle, the check will say so and
the fix belongs in this slice, not later.

### 2.2 The agent endpoints

`/en/cv.json` and `/en/llms.txt`. Both are already generated from a resolved
view, so they follow the dataset; the only work is the route and passing the
locale. `endpoints.check.ts` runs over both.

This is the part with the clearest payoff: the crawlers and agents that read a
CV in English are exactly the ones these endpoints exist for.

### 2.3 The OG card

`public/og.en.jpg`, generated by the same `build-og.ts`, with the texts derived
from the English dataset. `og.lock.json` grows to fingerprint both images, so
`test:og` fails if either goes stale.

The card's geometry, photo and brand come from `src/lib/brand.ts` and do not
fork. `og-output.check.ts` already verifies dimensions, WhatsApp's weight ceiling
and that the favicon still draws the same ring; it gains a second image, not a
second implementation.

### 2.4 `hreflang`

On both landings:

```html
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="es" href="https://…/" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="en" href="https://…/en/" />
<link rel="alternate" hreflang="x-default" href="https://…/" />
```

`x-default` points at Spanish: the market is LatAm, and `x-default` is what a
reader in a third language gets.

`/cv` and `/en/cv` get nothing: they are `noindex`. Emitting `hreflang` on a page
that asks not to be indexed is a contradiction crawlers report as an error.

`docs/09` §2.3 gets rewritten from "why not, and when to reconsider" to what is
emitted — that file's whole premise is that the absences are decided, so an
absence that has been filled has to stop being listed as a decision.

`@astrojs/sitemap` needs its `i18n` option so the sitemap declares the same
relations. A sitemap that disagrees with the tags is worse than neither.

### 2.5 The switch

An `ES / EN` control in the header of both landings, linking to the same section
on the other side when the anchor has one — `#proyectos` ↔ `#projects` — and to
the top otherwise. It is a link, not a script: `/` ships JS, but the switch must
work without it, and `/cv` is at zero JS and stays there.

No automatic redirect by `Accept-Language`. It breaks the back button, it hides
Spanish from a reader who wants it, and on a static host it costs a redirect rule
in front of every request.

## 3. What must NOT drift

The failure mode of a bilingual site is two sites. Three concrete guards, all of
which exist by the end of slice 1 and are extended here rather than invented:

| Guard | Extended how |
|---|---|
| `i18n.check.ts` | Structure parity already covers the dataset. Here it also covers the anchor table: the same set of section ids in both locales |
| `single-landing.check.ts` | Both landings are the only doors: `/cv` and `/en/cv` stay `noindex` and link-free, and each landing's CV section matches its own PDF |
| `no-client-js.check.ts` | `PAGES_WITH_JS` grows by exactly one entry: `en/index.html`. Adding a page to that allowlist stays an explicit line in a diff |

## 4. Order of work

1. The anchor table + the landing as a locale-taking component. Spanish output
   byte-identical; the existing checks are the proof.
2. `/en/` and `PAGES_WITH_JS`.
3. `/en/cv.json`, `/en/llms.txt`, `endpoints.check.ts`.
4. The OG card and `og.lock.json`.
5. `hreflang`, the sitemap's `i18n`, and the rewrite of `docs/09` §2.3.
6. The switch.

Steps 3 and 4 are independent of each other and of 5; step 6 needs 2.

## 5. Deliberately out of scope

- **A third language.** The design makes it a file plus a dictionary column, and
  that is as far as it should go until there is a reason.
- **Translating `docs/`.** The repo is written in English already, and the docs
  are for whoever works on it, not for the reader.
- **Case studies and the freelance surfaces.** They are not built in either
  language yet; building them twice before they exist once is backwards.
