# Let the work introduce the person

Synthesis of the notes in `docs/v2/research_notes/Marca personal para desarrollador web/`, written 2026-10-26 for the v2 landing. Only claims carried by a source in those notes are cited. Where the notes found no evidence, this report says so.

A developer who wants to come across as a person rather than a company should make the evidence do the selling and keep the voice plain. In a 2025–2026 market where AI makes polished text and code nearly free, the scarce signal is **specificity that is costly to fake**: named clients, real dates, a traced number marked as estimated, a decision and the alternative it rejected. Claims and adjectives cost nothing to produce, so they carry almost no information. That is the core of signaling theory, and recent hiring-platform experiments support it. The research also sets two clocks. Visual appeal is judged in about 50 ms, and the decision to leave a web page is concentrated in the first 10 seconds. The "wow" therefore has to be a fast, legible first paint followed by **one engineered peak and a deliberate ending**, not a long run of effects. The usability evidence against scroll hijacking is strong: most test participants were disoriented, task-focused users were the most annoyed, and mobile made it worse. That does not overturn the author's decision. It sets the engineering conditions that decision has to meet. A portfolio is mostly a tie-breaker and depth amplifier for employers, and closer to a gate for freelance clients, who buy on trust and social proof. Two gaps cannot be fixed with design. The testimonials collection is empty, and that is the largest freelance gap. English at A2 is the largest nearshore gap. The landing should be honest about both rather than paper over them. The concrete recommendation, set out in the last section, is a six-chapter arc. The fast path to the CV is visible from the first viewport. The first proof (the RPPL map) arrives as the second section and acts as the peak. The scroll ends with the opening motif converging on the CV download.

## Specificity is the costly signal in an AI-flooded market

The case for "show, don't tell" is not only taste. It is the oldest result in the economics of hiring. Since Spence (1973), a signal has been considered credible when it is **cheaper for a high-quality sender to produce than for a low-quality one to imitate** ([Wikipedia: Signalling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signalling_(economics))). MIT IDE's field experiments on hiring platforms found that a costly badge raised employer invites by about 4.8% per impression, and that renters still received **about 50% more invites** two years later. The authors' line is the thesis of this report: "a signal that anyone can claim at any time for free will be claimed by everyone and mean nothing" ([MIT IDE](https://ide.mit.edu/insights/can-costly-signals-fix-hiring/)). Applying this to personal websites is a theoretical extension, not a tested finding, but nothing in the notes contradicts it.

The market has made this sharper. **84% of developers use or plan to use AI tools**, yet only about a third trust their accuracy, and the top frustration (66%) is output that is "almost right, but not quite" ([Stack Overflow Survey 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)). Recruiters report that résumés are "clearly tailored by AI to mirror the application". One fintech recruiter puts inbound hires at **around 10%** of the total, and managers say references remain the best channel ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-tech-market-in-2025-hiring-managers)). HackerRank's 2025 survey of 13,732 respondents frames hiring as "real ability, not resume keywords". Its own data shows hiring growth concentrated at lead (+22%) and senior (+19%) levels against about 7% at entry level. HackerRank sells assessments, so its framing serves its product ([HackerRank 2025](https://www.hackerrank.com/reports/developer-skills-report-2025)). Stanford's payroll study finds employment of 22–25-year-olds in AI-exposed occupations **19% below trend**, mainly because fewer are hired, while experienced workers show no comparable gap ([Stanford Digital Economy Lab](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/)).

For a developer working since 2020 this cuts both ways. The author sits in the band the data shows is not shrinking, but only if the page reads as experienced (scope, ownership, decisions) and not as "a junior with more years". The positioning that serves both job and freelance audiences comes from McKenzie's practitioner essay: describe yourself by what you accomplished, not by stack ("anyone describing themselves as either a Java programmer or .NET programmer has already lost") ([kalzumeus.com](https://www.kalzumeus.com/2011/10/28/dont-call-yourself-a-programmer/)). The niche literature (Morgan, Stark) is practitioner consensus with known trade-offs, not evidence ([Business of Freelancing](https://freelancing.transistor.fm/episodes/positioning-101-for-freelancers-w-philip-morgan); [Jonathan Stark](https://jonathanstark.com/daily/20170704-problems-with-specializing-on-a-vertical-market)). An industry niche would narrow the job side. A **problem niche** works on both surfaces: interfaces that cannot fail under heavy data, meaning maps with thousands of features and checkouts that cannot lose a seat. The dataset already holds that line in its own voice (`summary.long`: "Si el producto no puede fallar en un checkout o en un mapa con miles de features, ahí me siento cómodo") and evidence behind it (RPPL map, WooCommerce Seat Map, AsyncPile).

### Adaptability is a dated trail, not an adjective

"I adapt to new technologies" is exactly the kind of free claim that signals nothing. The credible version is visible, dated output. swyx calls it "learning exhaust" ([swyx](https://www.swyx.io/learn-in-public)), and Bock's résumé formula asks for an accomplishment "measured by Y, by doing Z", with a baseline ([Business Insider](https://www.businessinsider.in/google-hr-boss-says-this-is-the-key-to-a-perfect-resume/articleshow/43924179.cms)). Structurally, that means showing when and why each tool entered the work: agency landings in Vue and Django, a Vue 3 audience builder in adtech, Mapbox at two employers, then Vite, Turborepo, WooCommerce internals and AI-assisted work. Each entry needs a date and one piece of evidence. The `Skill.periods` field is the natural source, and its empty periods are data to fill, not to invent. There is a second, stronger proof of adaptation, provided it is narrated as decisions and not as a feature list: v2 is itself a rebuild on a stack (GSAP, ScrollTrigger, possibly WebGPU-era three.js) that the v1 author had deliberately constrained.

AI fluency needs the same treatment. Employers increasingly list it as a requirement, but in one vendor survey only **26% require candidates to demonstrate AI use and verify the results** ([TestGorilla](https://www.testgorilla.com/blog/state-of-hiring-for-ai-fluency-2026/)). Among professionals, **72% say vibe coding is not part of their work** ([Stack Overflow Survey 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)). Osmani's "70% problem" names the scarce skill: the last stretch of edge cases, integration and security "requires the kind of engineering judgment that AI can't reliably provide" ([Osmani](https://addyosmani.com/agentic-engineering/the-70-percent-problem/)). The stance the data supports is **"I use AI daily; I own the verification"**. The proof is already in the repository: CI gates for the PDF, the JS policy, i18n staleness, and a content contract that rejects hand-written durations. "Built with AI" or a list of tool names reads as outsourced thinking. "What I delegated, what the gate caught, what I rejected" reads as judgment. Freelance buyers need a softer version, because **41% of businesses are skeptical of AI tools** ([Small Business Trends on Upwork](https://smallbiztrends.com/upwork-future-workforce-index-2025-freelancer-growth/)). For them, AI is a speed benefit with quality control, never the headline.

## Three readers arrive with ten seconds and a prior opinion

The hero faces two tests. Lindgaard et al. found homepage visual-appeal ratings after **50 ms** matched ratings after longer viewing ([Nature](https://www.nature.com/news/2006/060109/full/news060109-13.html)). Willis and Todorov showed that extra time mainly **raises confidence in a first impression rather than revising it** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16866745/)). The second test is time. In Liu, White and Dumais's 2 billion dwell times, the chance of leaving is highest at the start. NN/g summarizes: "the first 10 seconds of the page visit are critical", and a page that holds about 30 seconds often keeps the visitor for more than two minutes ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-long-do-users-stay-on-web-pages/)). The implication for an animated landing is specific. The 50 ms verdict is delivered by the **first paint**, not by an animation that has not loaded yet, and later chapters rarely overturn a weak opening. They confirm it.

The readers are three, and they want different things. **Recruiters** filter on surface legibility: title, seniority, stack, location, time zone, a clean layout and a downloadable CV. Ladders' eye-tracking puts the initial résumé screen at **7.4 s** and penalizes clutter and multiple columns ([HR Dive](https://www.hrdive.com/news/eye-tracking-study-shows-recruiters-look-at-resumes-for-7-seconds/541582/)). Critics note this is a filter time, not an evaluation time ([ERE](https://www.ere.net/articles/is-the-6-second-resume-scan-a-myth)). **Hiring managers** look for depth: one project sustained over months, decisions and trade-offs, team framing, business problems over framework lists ([Ask HN](https://hn.svelte.dev/item/43134745)). **Freelance clients** decide on trust. In GoDaddy's survey of 204 US small-business owners, **0% would hire a provider with neither reviews nor a portfolio**, 64% distrust a provider with no reviews, 73% find providers through recommendations, and no interviewee chose on price. "Vision" (understanding their business) ranked first ([GoDaddy](https://www.godaddy.com/resources/news/what-clients-want)). That data is US-only, from 2022, and from a vendor. The notes found no LatAm small-business data.

Two consequences follow. First, the site is mostly a **verification destination**. A Profy.dev survey (secondhand, via Scrimba) found **93% of hiring managers would open a portfolio, yet 51% said a candidate's chances would not drop without one** ([Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/how-to-build-a-web-developer-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired)). Most visitors therefore arrive after a CV, a LinkedIn profile or a referral has already earned the click. The landing's job is to confirm and deepen that impression, and to make whoever referred the developer look good for doing so. Second, the CV is the artifact that circulates: screeners forward the PDF, not the experience. The experience has to make the person memorable and the download frictionless, and it never replaces the CV. This fits the decision to keep the PDF pages unchanged. The single-column, zero-JS CV is supported by the Ladders layout findings, not only by ATS parsing.

### What design cannot fill: testimonials and English

The empty `testimonials` collection is the **single highest-leverage missing element for freelance work**, per the GoDaddy figures. It must stay empty until real testimonials exist. The landing should therefore not reserve a visual slot for them: an empty or placeholder "what clients say" block is worse than none. Until real ones arrive, the honest substitutes for social proof are verifiable names (RPPL, Muse CRM, Dinkum Interactive, Adsmovil, Hogarth), which are authority by evidence rather than by claim. Asking two or three past clients or leads for a real sentence is a data task, not a design task.

English at **A2** is the material gap for US/EU remote buyers, whose top objection in the (vendor-heavy) nearshore literature is communication friction. Their other objections are overstated seniority ("Resumes lie, or at minimum, they overstate"), reliability and contractor compliance ([Revelo](https://www.revelo.com/blog/latam-vs-eastern-europe-vs-asia-why-latin-america-wins-for-us-remote-engineering-teams-in-2026)). Argentina leads Latin America on EF's 2025 index (26th of 123) ([Mercado](https://mercado.com.ar/noticias/argentina-alcanza-el-puesto-26-en-el-indice-de-proficiencia-en-ingles-de-ef-education-first)). That national halo does not transfer to an individual with A2. There is a trap the notes do not name explicitly but that follows from their logic: **a polished English `/en/` site implies an English level that an A2 interview will contradict**, and an inconsistency discovered late costs more than a limit stated early. The `/en/` surface should state the level plainly next to the contact path, keep its copy short and literal, and make the other objections cheap to dismiss with facts. Rosario is UTC-3: one to two hours ahead of US Eastern depending on daylight saving time, and four to five hours behind Central Europe. Tenure and continuity can be shown through dated roles. Answering the identity concern also matters, because full-remote startups report applicants who spoof their locations ([Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/state-of-the-tech-market-in-2025-hiring-managers)). A real name on a personal domain with consistent GitHub and LinkedIn history is a quiet trust signal.

## Wow comes from one concept, one peak and a designed ending

The award-level portfolios of 2025–2026 share a pattern: **a single strong concept, not a pile of effects**. Bruno Simon says of his career-launching 2019 portfolio: "Nothing groundbreaking. Yet it went viral and jump-started my career." His 2025 rebuild is a world, built with instancing, culling, compressed textures and a lower mobile preset, because "if the experience runs at 10 FPS, no one will stick around" ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). Joseph Santamaria's scroll-driven 3D portfolio aimed to "feel like a place rather than a document", with each scene composed like a film frame, after about three and a half months of work ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=114641)). The closest precedent to this project's stack is Joffrey Spitzer's featured build: **Astro + GSAP + Lenis + one three.js element**, "minimal" with "subtle motion" ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/02/18/joffrey-spitzer-portfolio-a-minimalist-astro-gsap-build-with-reveals-flip-transitions-and-subtle-motion/)). It shows that "minimal plus one motion signature" is a recognized, award-level register, not a compromise. The tooling cost has disappeared too: GSAP, including SplitText and MorphSVG, is free for commercial use, and the rewritten SplitText has screen-reader accessibility built in ([Webflow](https://webflow.com/blog/gsap-becomes-free); [Webflow forum](https://discourse.webflow.com/t/webflow-makes-gsap-100-free/319967)).

Careers built on spectacle exist. Jesse Zhou's three.js portfolio "attracted multiple job offers" ([Zero to Mastery](https://zerotomastery.io/about/instructor/jesse-zhou)). But those are creative-developer careers, where the spectacle is the work sample. For product-company screening the advice runs the other way. A developer who screened 200+ applicants writes "the simpler your portfolio website, the better" ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/etienne-dev/just-launched-my-personal-portfolio-3gka/comments)), and Scrimba says to make the reviewer's seconds count "with strong project work, not flashy animations" ([Scrimba](https://scrimba.com/articles/how-to-build-a-web-developer-portfolio-that-gets-you-hired)). No study measures animated or 3D portfolios against hiring outcomes. The defensible synthesis is that **the experience earns its place only when the animation discloses evidence**: a map that draws itself from the geospatial work, not decoration that sits next to it.

Two psychological effects shape where the effort goes. The **peak-end rule** says experiences are remembered by their most intense moment and their ending, and that duration barely matters ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/)). Axess Lab argues for investing in the peak and the ending, not only the first screen ([Axess Lab](https://axesslab.com/flipped-web-design/)). The **aesthetic-usability effect** buys forgiveness, but "for minor usability problems, but not of large ones" ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/aesthetic-usability-effect/)). Confusion and slow loading become the remembered negative peak ([Uxcel](https://app.uxcel.com/lessons/ux-laws-999/peak-end-rule-6768)). Combined with the front-loaded exit risk from the dwell-time data, this argues for a conclusion the notes imply but do not state: **put the peak second, not last**. Memory is dominated by the peak whenever it occurs, while the risk of losing the visitor is highest in the first 30 seconds. The first proof should therefore arrive early and be the strongest moment, and the ending should reuse its visual language so the CV download reads as a resolution rather than a footer. That last technique is inferred from peak-end and the "film frame" framing; no source documents it.

On a developer's site, craft details are not decoration: they *are* the work sample, and the aesthetic halo transfers to the person. The 2002 Stanford credibility study found "design look" the most-mentioned credibility factor (46.1% of comments) ([ClickZ](https://clickz.com/study-for-site-cred-looks-matter/56564/)). The same effect turns negative fast. A stalled loader, a spinning laptop fan or a CV hidden behind a sequence is a "large" problem that beauty does not offset. Restraint is itself a signal here: a 3D or WebGL layer that degrades cleanly says more about seniority than one more shader.

## Scroll hijacking is accepted, so its known failure modes become requirements

The decision stands. The evidence defines what it must survive. NN/g's testing found that **"the majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented by scrolljacking"**. Some left or refreshed. Pages that changed both scroll rate and direction while asking the user to read did worst, mobile made sequences longer and more disorienting, and task-focused users were the most annoyed: one said he "would get severely agitated and just move on". The same study names when it works: scrolljacks "were best tolerated when they were used to progressively disclose relevant information". They should be short, interleaved with normal scrolling, never change scroll direction, keep text minimal, sit **below the fold so they do not hide key content or calls to action**, and offer sticky navigation as an escape ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). A Purdue thesis adds that readers who scroll a little and read a few lines tend to be "blind" to parallax entirely ([Purdue](https://docs.lib.purdue.edu/open_access_theses/1616)). Effects pitched at skimmers are partly wasted.

Recruiters are the task-focused user NN/g describes, often on a corporate laptop with an integrated GPU or blocked WebGL. The notes found no data on enterprise WebGL failure rates, which is itself a reason to design for the worst case. Each risk has a matching mitigation that does not reverse the decision:

| Risk (evidence) | Mitigation that keeps the experience |
|---|---|
| CTAs hidden inside sequences ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)) | CV download and contact present in the first viewport, as plain HTML that works before any JS loads. The hero is never part of a scrolljack. |
| No escape once the header is removed ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)) | A minimal persistent control (logo ring, "CV", language) instead of a full header. It answers NN/g's finding that sticky navigation helped users escape. |
| Disorientation from direction changes | Vertical gestures produce vertical-feeling motion. No horizontal-scroll chapters. |
| Long sequences read as broken | Pinned chapters of a few viewport heights, separated by normal-scroll reading sections. Tune the scroll rate by testing on real devices. |
| Vestibular reactions: "dizziness, nausea and headaches" ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)) | `prefers-reduced-motion` disables Lenis, pinning and scrubbing outright and shows the static composition of each chapter, not a slowed-down version. |
| Mobile amplifies everything ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)) | A mobile-specific version. Native touch scrolling, lighter scenes, and a quality preset like Simon's ([Awwwards](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). |
| Smooth scroll replaces native physics | Lenis is the component most likely to produce "this site is fighting me". Builders adopt it to sync animation, not for the user ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=114934)). Trial it against native scroll with ScrollTrigger pinning, and keep keyboard (Space, PageDown, Home, End) and anchor navigation working. |
| A loader as the remembered negative peak | No full-screen percentage loader. Animation reveals content that is already painted. |
| Kinetic type read letter by letter | SplitText's built-in accessibility. Real text stays in the DOM. |
| SEO, ATS and agent readers | The static HTML is the baseline and the experience is an enhancement. `/cv`, `/cv.json` and `/llms.txt` stay independent of it. |

OpenReplay's warning sums up the stakes: animations "can also be what destroys your portfolio's chances with a recruiter" ([OpenReplay](https://blog.openreplay.com/prepare-for-success--create-a-modern-portfolio/)). The fast path to the CV is what makes accepting the hijack safe.

## A person without a photo is built from voice, consistency and candor

Without a face, the cue people judge trustworthiness from in about 100 ms ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/your-trustworthiness-judged-tenth-second-or-less)), the person has to be inferred from the voice and the work. The authenticity research says what makes a "human brand" read as genuine. Moulard, Garrity and Rice (n=428) found that **rarity (distinctiveness) and stability (consistency over time)** raise perceived authenticity ([LSU](https://repository.lsu.edu/marketing_pubs/49)), and a footballer study found **consistency and candidness** the strongest drivers ([Gdańsk Tech](https://pub.pg.edu.pl/pl/download/145864-personal-brand-authenticity-and-social-media-the-top-5-football-players-case)). The project's voice test ("could someone else with my stack have written this exact sentence?") is in effect a rarity test, which gives it a research footing. Stability comes almost for free from the single-source data model: the same facts appear on the landing, the CV, LinkedIn and the agent endpoints.

The tone between claim and modesty is also settled by evidence. Humblebragging, meaning bragging disguised as a complaint or as modesty, **lowers liking and perceived sincerity and fails to signal the trait it aims at** ([Sezer, Gino & Norton](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Sezer_Misguided-Self-Presentation.pdf)). A straight statement does better ([HBS Working Knowledge](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/humblebragging-is-a-bad-strategy-especially-in-a-job-interview)). The pratfall effect, where a small flaw makes someone more likeable, works **only for someone already seen as competent**, and it rests on a 1966 sample of about 48 students with no modern replication ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratfall_effect)). The landing can be candid about limits (A2 English, "what I'd do differently" in a case) only after the proof, never in the opening. Countersignalling suggests that high-quality senders separate themselves by not shouting ([conceptually.org](https://conceptually.org/concepts/signalling-and-countersignalling)). That means no "hire me", no skill bars, and no scarcity unless it is true. This is theory, not tested on portfolios.

The cultural evidence supports one register across both languages, with different emphasis. Canadian recruiters favored self-promoting applicants more than Swiss recruiters did, and self-promotion worked by raising perceived competence ([UNIGE](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:101515)). The idea that Latin American audiences prefer modesty rests on practitioner opinion ([Molinsky](https://www.andymolinsky.com/?p=341)) and a US-Latina dissertation, not on LatAm samples. The approach that works on both ends of the spectrum is to **let artifacts and numbers do the self-promotion and keep the prose plain**. For `/en/`, under-claiming is the likelier failure against North American readers. For `/`, the same facts can carry less first-person weight. The facts cannot differ, and the i18n parity check already enforces that. Choosing rioplatense voseo or neutral Spanish has no research behind it. Stability argues for keeping the voice the dataset already uses ("laburé", "no me acuerdo los clientes y no los invento").

StoryBrand supplies a useful frame, despite being untested doctrine with critics ([Gravity Global](https://www.gravityglobal.com/blog/complete-guide-storybrand-framework); [Yu-kai Chou](https://yukaichou.com/?p=52664)): the reader is the hero and the developer is the guide. Here the reader is either a team that needs to ship something or a business with a problem, so the hero line should name their problem, not the developer's passion. Loewenstein's information-gap theory adds the pacing tool. Curiosity comes from a gap the reader notices **and believes can be closed** ([PBS NOVA](https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/article/how-to-stimulate-curiosity/)), so the opening should pose something the next scroll answers.

## Implications for the landing: arc, first two sections, copy rules

### Narrative structure

The arc below applies every finding above to the author's constraints: one continuous scroll, no photo, logo ring and dark tone as anchors, PDF unchanged, data read through `getView("portfolio")`. Each chapter's text comes from the dataset. Durations come from `careerStart` through `format.ts`, never typed by hand.

| # | Chapter | Job | Primary reader | Evidence from the dataset | Motion register |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Apertura | Pass the 50 ms and 10 s tests; open the curiosity gap; fast path to the CV | Recruiter | `identity` (searchable title, brand title, location, UTC-3), derived seniority, a rarity-tested hook | Content painted before JS; an entrance reveal; no hijack |
| 2 | Primera prueba: el mapa | The peak. Demonstrate the geospatial specialty, do not claim it | Hiring manager, technical client | `mapas-distritos`, `dinkum-mapbox` (~13.000 distritos, estimated), `dinkum-mapbox-index` (3–5 s → ~500 ms, estimated), `dinkum-mapbox-fix` | The one long pinned sequence; progressive disclosure, vertical only |
| 3 | Segunda prueba: nada se pierde | Modernizing legacy without breaking it, the freelance-relevant proof | Freelance client, hiring manager | `wp-plugins`, `dinkum-woo-sync` (AsyncPile), `muse-crm`, `muse-api` (rake with dry-run) | Mostly normal scroll; one short scrubbed detail |
| 4 | Recorrido | Adaptability as a dated trail | All three | `roles` (freelance 2020 → Adsmovil → Hogarth concurrent → Dinkum), `Skill.periods`, achievements per role | Timeline that reveals as it scrolls; native scroll |
| 5 | Cómo trabajo | AI-assisted, human-governed; honest limits after competence | Hiring manager | This site as artifact (CI gates, zero-JS CV, measured vs estimated, `TODO` instead of invented numbers); English A2 stated plainly | Calm; little or no animation |
| 6 | Cierre | Resolve the opening motif into the CV download; contact | All three | PDF (`Cribb_Nicolas_CV_<updatedAt>.pdf`), email, GitHub, LinkedIn | The opening motif converges on the download control |

Two absences are deliberate. There is no testimonials chapter until real testimonials exist, and no services grid while `services` is empty. For freelance readers, the close carries one sentence in the "vision" register GoDaddy found clients want (understanding the business before the screen) and a contact path, not an offer menu.

### Section 1: Apertura

The first viewport must be complete and beautiful as **static HTML and CSS before any script runs**, because that is what the 50 ms verdict sees and what a locked-down corporate laptop gets. It holds the logo ring, the name, "Desarrollador Full Stack" as the searchable title, then "Product Engineer" (docs/02's order: searchable first, identity after), the derived years of experience, "Rosario, Argentina · UTC-3", one hook sentence, and two plain controls: download the CV (PDF) and contact. The language switch lives in the persistent control that replaces the header. That is the recruiter's whole 10-second need, and it does not depend on the experience loading.

The hook sentence is where the voice test matters most. The current tagline, "Construyo productos web completos, no solo la interfaz.", is true, but another full-stack developer could have written it. A rarity-tested hook names the problem niche and the reader's stakes, in the territory of `summary.long`: products that cannot fail under heavy data, maps with thousands of features, checkouts that cannot lose a seat. The author has to write the final words, and it must stay within 180 characters if it lives in a `Prose.short`. The open loop is visual. The logo ring is the one object that has motion from the start: a slow, quiet hint that it will become something. Scrolling then answers what it becomes, which is the closeable gap Loewenstein describes. There is no full-screen loader and no scroll lock in this section. Motion here is an entrance reveal of content that is already painted.

### Section 2: Primera prueba, el mapa

Section 2 is the first scroll-driven chapter and the designed peak. The case for the RPPL map: it is the only work with **two traced metrics** in the dataset. It is the geospatial differentiator docs/02 identifies. And it carries a real engineering decision, not just an output. The ring from section 1 opens into a map that draws itself as the reader scrolls. Each pinned beat discloses one fact in one line: who needed it (school leaders exploring spending on teacher training); the scale (**~13.000 distritos**, estimated, so it renders with "~"); what broke (paint artifacts in districts with no data, hover at extreme zoom); the decision (the feature-state merge replaced by a hash index in a Web Worker); and the outcome (**indexing from 3–5 s to ~500 ms**, estimated, with the "~" applied by `formatMetric`, never typed). This is NN/g's tolerated case exactly: a short sequence that progressively discloses relevant information, with minimal text, vertical only, below the fold, releasing into normal scroll when it ends.

Three constraints apply. First, confirm the client permits showing their visualization or data shape. If it does not, draw generated geometry that is honestly labelled as illustrative and keep the real numbers in the text. Second, keep the sequence to a few viewport heights and test the scroll rate on a mid-range phone and an integrated-GPU laptop, because this is where the "10 FPS, no one will stick around" rule bites. Third, the reduced-motion and low-capability version of this chapter is the same five beats laid out as a static figure with text. It is not a blank space and not a slower animation. This chapter also sets the visual vocabulary (line weight, accent, easing) that chapter 6 reuses when the motif converges on the CV.

### Copy rules

| Rule | Why (evidence) | In practice |
|---|---|---|
| Every sentence passes the rarity test: a proper name, a number or a decision | Rarity drives perceived authenticity ([LSU](https://repository.lsu.edu/marketing_pubs/49)); adjectives are free signals ([MIT IDE](https://ide.mit.edu/insights/can-costly-signals-fix-hiring/)) | "Reemplacé el merge en feature-state por un índice hash en un Web Worker", not "optimicé el rendimiento" |
| No adjectives about the person | Claims are cheap; countersignalling ([conceptually.org](https://conceptually.org/concepts/signalling-and-countersignalling)) | No "apasionado", "detallista", "experto", and docs/02's banned list throughout |
| Name the reader's problem before the developer's skill | StoryBrand's guide-not-hero move ([Gravity Global](https://www.gravityglobal.com/blog/complete-guide-storybrand-framework)) | Open each proof chapter with who needed what |
| Numbers only from the dataset, estimates with "~" via `formatMetric`, never typed | Specificity survives interviews; invented numbers collapse | `TODO` stays in the data and never becomes a headline: pick chapters whose data is complete |
| State, don't humblebrag | Humblebrags lower liking and sincerity ([Sezer et al.](https://marketing.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Sezer_Misguided-Self-Presentation.pdf)) | Plain past tense, active verb, object, outcome (docs/02 pattern) |
| Limits stated plainly, and only after the proof | Pratfall helps only the already-competent ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratfall_effect)) | "Inglés: A2" near the contact path on `/en/`; one "what I'd change" line inside a case |
| AI framed as owned verification, never as the headline | Low trust in AI output; 72% reject vibe coding ([Stack Overflow 2025](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)) | "Uso IA a diario; lo que entra al repo pasa por gates que escribí" |
| Text inside pinned sequences: one line per beat | NN/g: minimal text in scrolljacks; it also halves ES/EN parity cost | Long explanation goes in normal-scroll sections or `Prose.long` |
| Same facts, register tuned per language | Self-promotion tolerance varies by culture ([UNIGE](https://archive-ouverte.unige.ch/unige:101515)) | `/en/` direct first person, short and literal; `/` same facts, the dataset's rioplatense voice |
| No "hire me", no false scarcity, no skill bars, no logo wall | Scarcity unvalidated and risky; bars are self-rated and unverifiable | Availability is stated only when true, and as a fact |
| The CV is offered, not pushed: visible from the first viewport, climactic at the end | The PDF is what circulates; peak-end ([Laws of UX](https://lawsofux.com/peak-end-rule/)) | One label used consistently ("Descargar CV" / "Download CV"), the same control both times |

## Conclusion

The research reframes the author's two big decisions as one bet. Accepting scroll hijacking and removing the photo both push the burden of "who is this person" onto the content of each scene, and that suits a profile whose real assets are traceable specifics: a 13,000-district map, an index that went from seconds to half a second, a seat-reservation queue that keeps WooCommerce from losing state. Animation is the right medium for such facts only when it discloses them. The design question for every chapter is therefore not "is this impressive?" but "what fact does this motion reveal?". The answer is also what keeps the experience from reading as a creative-developer pitch to a product-company recruiter.

The binding constraints are not visual. The two largest conversion gaps, real testimonials and English beyond A2, sit outside the landing, and the strongest acquisition channel, referrals, sits outside the site. The landing can make a referral convert and a CV get forwarded, and that is the right ambition for it. Spending the next effort on two or three real client sentences and filled-in `Skill.periods` will likely move outcomes more than any additional shader. That is an inference, but every source in these notes points the same way.
